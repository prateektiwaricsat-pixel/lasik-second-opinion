Why a cornea specialist

The person who treats LASIK complications is the right person to screen you.

LASIK is safe for the right eye. The problems come from the wrong eye — a thin or weak cornea, early keratoconus, unstable power or untreated dry eye. Those are exactly the conditions a cornea specialist treats every day, including the rare patients who develop corneal ectasia after refractive surgery.

Independent

You can get operated anywhere — including the centre that first counselled you. Our job is only to tell you whether the plan is right for your eyes.

Evidence-based

We read your actual corneal maps and measurements, not just your glasses number.

In writing

You receive a written summary you can show your surgeon, your family, or keep for your records.

Reports to upload

What we need from you

These are your reports — your LASIK centre should share them if you ask. Clear phone photos are fine.

  • Corneal topography / tomography (Pentacam, Sirius, Galilei, Orbscan or similar) — all maps, both eyes
  • Corneal thickness (pachymetry), if done separately
  • Current glasses or contact lens prescription, and an older one from 1–2 years ago if you have it
  • What the centre recommended — procedure name and quotation
  • Optional: dry eye tests, aberrometry, ICL sizing scans
What we look at

The questions that matter

  • Is your cornea thick and strong enough for the planned procedure?
  • Any early signs of keratoconus or ectasia risk?
  • Has your glasses power been stable long enough?
  • Is LASIK the best option — or would SMILE, PRK or ICL be safer for you?
  • Does dry eye need treatment before surgery?
When a second opinion is most useful

Pause and get a second look if…

  • You were told the price before anyone explained your scan results
  • You were offered a discount that "expires today"
  • Nobody showed you your corneal map or explained it
  • Only one procedure was discussed, with no alternatives
  • You were told you have "thin cornea" or "borderline" results but still advised surgery
  • You simply want to be sure before a decision you can't undo

No. This is a report-based specialist opinion. The final decision on surgery is always made by your operating surgeon after examining your eyes in person.

No. Everything happens online — upload, payment, video call and written summary. If you later want surgery or further tests with us, that is entirely your choice.
